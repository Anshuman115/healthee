## Finding
Caffeine consumed at **bedtime, 3 hours before bed, or 6 hours before bed**
all measurably reduce total sleep time and increase wake time after sleep
onset, compared with placebo. The effect is significant even at 6 hours before
bed and is detected both subjectively and by polysomnography.

## Effect size
- 400 mg caffeine 6 hours before bed: **~1 hour total sleep time reduction**
  vs placebo, measured by both polysomnography and home sleep recordings
  (Drake et al. 2013).

## Evidence strength
- Drake C, Roehrs T, Shambroom J, Roth T. **"Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed."** *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine* 2013;9(11):1195–1200. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, within-subject design.
- Clark I, Landolt HP. **"Coffee, caffeine, and sleep: A systematic review of epidemiological studies and randomized controlled trials."** *Sleep Medicine Reviews* 2017;31:70–78.

## Caveats
- Individual variability in caffeine metabolism is large (CYP1A2 genotype affects half-life, with population range ~3–7 hours).
- Habitual heavy users develop partial tolerance to sleep effects but not full tolerance.
- Dose matters; smaller doses (≤100 mg) have smaller effects but are not negligible 3–6 hours before bed.

## Operational use
- Flag caffeine entries logged within ~6 hours of typical bedtime as a likely
  contributor when sleep quality dips, citing this note.
- Do not flag morning-only caffeine as a sleep risk under normal circumstances.
- Help the user identify their personal cutoff window across multiple nights.
