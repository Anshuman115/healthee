## Finding
Slow-paced breathing at approximately **6 breaths per minute** (5.5–6 breaths/min,
or a 10-second cycle) **acutely** increases multiple HRV indices during and
shortly after the practice. The effect is mediated by resonance with baroreflex
oscillations and is reproducible in healthy adults across many studies.

The mechanism is well-characterized: slow breathing maximizes respiratory
sinus arrhythmia and engages parasympathetic ("vagal") tone.

## Effect size
- Acute increase in RMSSD: commonly **+30–80%** during a 10–20 minute slow-breathing session vs spontaneous breathing baseline (Laborde et al. 2017).
- Acute increase in HF power: typically 2–5× vs baseline.
- Effects largely return to baseline within minutes of stopping, **though sub-acute effects** (next 30–60 min) and **chronic effects** (with daily practice) are documented but smaller and less consistent.

## Evidence strength
- Laborde S, Allen MS, Borges U, et al. **"Effects of voluntary slow breathing on heart rate and heart rate variability: A systematic review and a meta-analysis."** *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 2022;138:104711. Meta-analysis confirming acute HRV increase.
- Russo MA, Santarelli DM, O'Rourke D. **"The physiological effects of slow breathing in the healthy human."** *Breathe* 2017;13(4):298–309. Mechanistic review.
- Laborde S, Mosley E, Thayer JF. **"Heart rate variability and cardiac vagal tone in psychophysiological research."** *Frontiers in Public Health* 2017;5:258. Methodological framework + summary.

## Caveats
- Most evidence is **acute** (within-session). Chronic-baseline HRV improvement from regular slow-breathing practice is plausible and supported by some RCTs but the effect size at the baseline level is smaller.
- The optimal pace varies slightly between individuals (4.5–7 breaths/min); ~6 is a population average.
- People with cardiopulmonary disease should not interpret HRV changes as health-grade.

## Operational use
- When the user logs a meditation/breathwork session, the **expected** HRV response in the following minutes/hours is increased RMSSD.
- A breathwork session **followed by elevated overnight HRV** is consistent with this evidence; report this honestly as expected, not as a surprise.
- Do not promise chronic baseline HRV shifts from short-term practice.
