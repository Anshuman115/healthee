## Finding
Time-domain HRV indices, particularly **RMSSD** measured during sleep or
controlled rest, are a validated marker of cardiac vagal tone and respond
predictably to acute stressors (training load, alcohol, illness, sleep
deprivation, late meals) and recovery interventions (sleep, slow breathing,
endurance fitness).

## Effect size
- Day-to-day **within-individual** RMSSD variability is meaningful: deviations
  of >1 SD from a 30-day rolling baseline are correlated with acute stressors
  the prior day.
- Group-level **between-individual** RMSSD differences correlate with fitness
  level, age, and cardiometabolic status, but the within-individual signal is
  what consumer wearable HRV is good for.

## Evidence strength
- Shaffer F, Ginsberg JP. **"An overview of heart rate variability metrics and norms."** *Frontiers in Public Health* 2017;5:258. Validated metric definitions.
- Plews DJ, Laursen PB, Stanley J, et al. **"Training adaptation and heart rate variability in elite endurance athletes: opening the door to effective monitoring."** *Sports Medicine* 2013;43(9):773–781. Standardized within-subject HRV monitoring.
- Kim HG, Cheon EJ, Bai DS, et al. **"Stress and heart rate variability: A meta-analytic review of the literature."** *Psychiatry Investigation* 2018;15(3):235–245.

## Caveats
- **Absolute RMSSD values are not comparable between people.** A nightly RMSSD of 35 ms may be low for one person and high for another.
- Posture, breathing rate, and measurement window heavily affect HRV. Comparisons should always be like-for-like (e.g., during sleep).
- Wrist-/strap-based wearable HRV is less accurate than ECG-derived HRV; trends are reliable, exact values are not.
- A single low-HRV night carries little signal; rolling averages (3–7 day) are more meaningful.

## Operational use
- Use **personal rolling baseline** (e.g., 28-day mean and SD) rather than absolute thresholds.
- Surface HRV deviations alongside known recent stressors (alcohol, late caffeine, poor sleep, hard exercise the day before) rather than as a stand-alone alarm.
- Never use HRV alone to indicate health status; cite this note for what it can and can't tell us.
