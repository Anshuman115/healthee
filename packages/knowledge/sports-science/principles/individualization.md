# Individualization — Training the Runner, Not the Population

## Summary
Two runners doing the identical program can adapt very differently: in controlled
trials VO₂max gains range from ~0 to >1.0 L/min, and roughly half of that
variability is genetic [Bouchard 1999]. Population guidelines (e.g. "easy =
70–80% HRmax", "10% weekly volume rule") are **starting estimates, not
prescriptions** — the runner's own response data is the higher-authority source.
The single most important coaching takeaway: **set defaults from the science,
then continuously re-anchor every threshold to this runner's measured baselines
and observed trajectory.** Apparent "non-response" is usually an under-dose or a
measurement artefact, not a fixed trait [Montero 2017; Pickering 2019].

## What it is
**Individualization** is the principle that training prescriptions must be tuned
to the specific athlete rather than applied uniformly from population averages. It
rests on three empirical facts:

1. **Inter-individual variability in trainability** — for the same dose of
   training, the *magnitude* of adaptation differs substantially between people.
2. **Genetic and biological moderation** — a meaningful share of that difference
   is heritable and trait-like, not just noise.
3. **Individual baselines** — physiological anchors (HRmax, lactate/ventilatory
   thresholds, resting HR, HRV, easy pace) vary so widely that a single
   percentage cut-off lands at different *internal* intensities for different
   runners [Mann 2013].

Practically, individualization means the coach treats every population default as
a **prior** to be updated by the runner's own data (an "n-of-1" stance), and
expresses confidence accordingly.

Typical ranges illustrate why one number cannot fit everyone:
- **VO₂max trainability**: from no measurable change up to +40–50% in extreme
  responders over months, with most untrained adults gaining ~15–20% [Bouchard
  1999; Bonafiglia 2022].
- **Heritability of the VO₂max *response*** to a standardized program: ~47%
  [Bouchard 1999].
- **Threshold heart rate** can sit anywhere from ~80% to ~92% of HRmax depending
  on the individual — so "% of HRmax" zones misclassify intensity for many
  runners [Mann 2013].

## Physiology / mechanism
Adaptation to endurance training is the integrated outcome of many partly
independent systems — cardiac output and plasma/haemoglobin volume expansion,
capillary and mitochondrial density, substrate handling, neuromuscular and
tendon remodelling — each with its own dose-response and its own genetic and
epigenetic regulation. Because these subsystems adapt at different rates and are
governed by different gene networks, two runners can reach the same race result
through different physiological routes, and respond differently to the same
stimulus.

In the HERITAGE Family Study, the VO₂max training response showed **2.5× more
variance between families than within families**, and the strongest single
determinant of improvement in dose-response work was the expansion of
**haemoglobin mass / blood volume** [Bouchard 1999; Montero 2017]. Candidate-gene
and genome-wide work has identified many small-effect variants associated with
trainability, but no validated single-gene predictor — the trait is highly
polygenic [Bonafiglia 2022; Pickering 2019].

Crucially, the dose-response curve itself is individual: a stimulus that is
supra-threshold (drives adaptation) for one runner can be sub-threshold (too
easy) for another. This is the mechanistic reason "non-responders" so often
become responders when the dose is raised [Montero 2017].

## The evidence

- **[Established]** Substantial inter-individual variability exists in the
  *magnitude* of adaptation to a standardized endurance program. In HERITAGE,
  481 previously sedentary adults completed an identical, fully-supervised
  20-week cycle program; mean VO₂max gain was ~400 ml/min but individual gains
  ranged from near-zero to >1.0 L/min [Bouchard 1999].

- **[Established]** A meaningful fraction of trainability is heritable. HERITAGE
  estimated **maximal heritability of the VO₂max response at ~47%** (age/sex
  adjusted), with familial aggregation far exceeding within-family variation
  [Bouchard 1999]. Population: previously sedentary white adults in two-generation
  families.

- **[Established]** Population-percentage intensity anchors (%HRmax, %VO₂max)
  produce **different internal/metabolic stress** in different individuals because
  the lactate/ventilatory threshold sits at a different %HRmax for each person;
  individualized (threshold- or RPE/talk-test-anchored) prescription is more
  equitable across athletes [Mann 2013].

- **[Probable]** "Non-response" is largely a function of **dose and measurement**,
  not a fixed trait. Montero & Lundby trained 78 adults at 1–5 sessions/week;
  cardiorespiratory non-responders fell from **69% → 40% → 29% → 0% → 0%** across
  ascending volumes, and non-response was **eliminated in every individual** when
  a previously low-dose group added 2 sessions/week for a further 6 weeks
  [Montero 2017, *J Physiol* 595(11):3377]. Narrative review reaches the same
  conclusion: truly universal non-responders are improbable [Pickering 2019].

- **[Probable]** Once you partition out **measurement error and day-to-day
  within-subject variability**, much of the apparent "individual response" can
  vanish. A true individual-response component is only demonstrated when the SD of
  change in the trained group exceeds that of a control group; many studies that
  claimed responder/non-responder subgroups never did this comparison [Atkinson
  2015; Bonafiglia 2022]. A large IPD meta-analysis (1,879 participants, 8 RCTs)
  found that conventional moderators (age, sex, baseline fitness) explained only a
  small part of CRF trainability variance [Bonafiglia 2022].

- **[Emerging]** **HRV-guided individualization** — timing hard sessions to days
  when vagally-mediated HRV (lnRMSSD) is at or above the runner's rolling
  baseline — can match or modestly beat a fixed plan. Vesterinen trained 40
  recreational runners; the HRV-guided group improved 3000 m time-trial pace
  (2.1%, P=0.004) while the predefined group did not significantly (1.1%, P=0.118)
  [Vesterinen 2016]. In well-trained cyclists, HRV-guided prescription beat block
  periodization on performance markers [Javaloyes 2019].

- **[Contested]** Whether HRV-guidance is *reliably superior* at the group level
  is unsettled. A meta-analysis (Manresa-Rocamora 2021) found only **trivial-to-
  small, non-significant** advantages for VO₂max (SMD +0.13) and performance (SMD
  +0.20); the one clearly significant effect was on vagal HRV itself (SMD +0.50).
  Interpretation: the *win* is mainly fewer wasted/maladaptive sessions and better
  individual timing, not a larger group mean.

- **[Established]** A minority can show **adverse responses** in specific
  cardiometabolic markers (not fitness) to standardized training. Pooling 6 RCTs,
  Bouchard found ~8–13% of individuals worsened on resting systolic BP, fasting
  insulin, triglycerides, or HDL beyond measurement error — underscoring that
  responses must be *monitored per-person*, not assumed [Bouchard 2012].

## How we compute it
Individualization is less a single metric than a **policy** the coach applies to
every other metric. Operational pieces (owned by `@daud/core`, several "not yet
computed"):

- **Individual baselines** — rolling personal anchors for resting HR, HRV
  (lnRMSSD 7-day mean + SD), easy pace at a given HR, threshold pace/HR, HRmax.
  These replace population defaults as soon as enough clean data exists
  (typically ≥2–4 weeks of consistent measurements).
- **Trainability estimate** — an *adaptive prior*, not a genetic test: observed
  change in fitness proxies (e.g. pace at fixed HR, HR at fixed pace, estimated
  VO₂max) per unit of training load over rolling 4–8 week windows. Used to tune
  expected response magnitude and progression rate.
- **Response tracking** — compares observed trajectory vs the expected band for
  the prescribed load. A flat or negative trend triggers the diagnostic ladder in
  *How the coach uses it* before any "non-responder" conclusion.

Estimation-error flag: field proxies (HR-at-pace, GPS pace, wrist HRV) carry real
noise. **Never declare non-response or a baseline shift from a single session.**
Require the signal to exceed typical within-subject day-to-day variation (use the
runner's own SD as the threshold), mirroring the Atkinson/Batterham logic
[Atkinson 2015].

## How the coach uses it
**Default → personalize → verify.** Start from the population value, then move
toward the runner's measured value as confidence grows; state confidence in
plain language matching the evidence grade.

- **Cold start (no history):** use population defaults from the relevant doc
  (zones, 10% volume rule, etc.) and *say so* — "I'm starting from typical values;
  I'll tune these to you over the next few weeks."
- **Anchor to measured baselines:** once available, prescribe intensity from the
  runner's own threshold/HRmax/easy-pace, not generic %-of-max bands [Mann 2013].
- **Apparent non-response → run the ladder, in order, before concluding anything:**
  1. **Check the dose** — is volume/intensity actually supra-threshold for *this*
     runner? Under-dosing is the most common cause [Montero 2017].
  2. **Check measurement** — is the change within day-to-day noise? Re-test
     [Atkinson 2015].
  3. **Check recovery/life load** — sleep, stress, fuelling, illness suppress
     adaptation and HRV.
  4. **Check modality fit** — vary the stimulus (more volume, or add intensity)
     before ever labelling the runner a non-responder [Pickering 2019].
- **HRV-guided timing (opt-in, Stage 2+):** when HRV is being tracked reliably,
  schedule key quality sessions on days at/above the runner's rolling lnRMSSD
  baseline and substitute easy/recovery when clearly suppressed — framed as
  *timing* support, with honest, modest expectations [Vesterinen 2016; Manresa-
  Rocamora 2021].

By stage:
- **Stage 1 (beginner):** lean on safe population defaults; collect baselines;
  variability is dominated by consistency and life factors, not genetics.
- **Stage 2 (developing):** baselines now exist — individualize zones, progression
  rate, and hard/easy day placement; begin trainability estimation.
- **Stage 3 (racing):** fully n-of-1 — progression, taper length, and session
  response are driven primarily by *this athlete's* historical response curves,
  with population science only as a sanity check.

## Honesty & uncertainty
- **Heritability ≠ destiny.** The ~47% figure is for the *response* to one
  standardized program in a specific population [Bouchard 1999]; it does not
  predict any individual's ceiling, and dose changes can move people between
  responder categories [Montero 2017].
- **The responder/non-responder construct is statistically fragile.** Without a
  control arm and repeated baseline measures, day-to-day noise alone can
  manufacture "responders" and "non-responders" that don't exist [Atkinson 2015;
  Bonafiglia 2022]. We treat individual classifications as provisional, never
  identity.
- **Field data is noisy.** Wrist HRV, GPS pace, and HR-at-pace drift with heat,
  sleep, hydration, caffeine, sensor placement and measurement timing. Trends over
  weeks are trustworthy; single readings are not.
- **HRV-guidance is promising but unsettled** at the group level — small,
  often-non-significant mean effects [Manresa-Rocamora 2021]. Its plausible value
  is reducing maladaptive sessions for *some* runners, and it depends heavily on
  standardized morning measurement; we present it as Emerging, not as a guarantee.
- **Most exercise-response research is on cycling/sedentary or clinical cohorts,
  not trained runners.** Effect sizes and "non-responder" rates may not transfer
  cleanly to a running-specific, already-trained athlete.
- **What's genuinely unknown:** there is **no validated genetic or blood test the
  coach can use to predict an individual runner's trainability** in advance
  [Pickering 2019]. We infer it only retrospectively from observed response.

## Safety bounds
- A small fraction (~8–13%) of people can show **adverse cardiometabolic
  responses** (e.g. rising resting BP) to otherwise-appropriate training
  [Bouchard 2012]. If tracked markers worsen persistently beyond noise, the coach
  must flag it and recommend professional/medical review — never push harder to
  "force" a response.
- Individualization must **never override hard safety guardrails** (e.g. load-ramp
  ceilings, pain/illness stop rules) in pursuit of a faster individual response.
  Personalization tunes *within* safety bounds, not past them.
- "Increase the dose to beat non-response" is bounded by the runner's recovery
  capacity and the acute:chronic load limits in the training-load doc; raise the
  stimulus *progressively*, not abruptly.

## Bottom line

- **Act on confidently (conclusive):**
  - The *magnitude* of adaptation to an identical program varies substantially
    between people (near-zero to >1.0 L/min VO₂max), and ~47% of the response is
    heritable — population averages cannot predict an individual's gain [Bouchard
    1999]. *Meta-/large-cohort grade.*
  - Population %-of-max intensity bands land at different internal stresses for
    different runners; once known, prescribe from the runner's own
    threshold/HRmax/easy-pace anchors [Mann 2013]. *Established.*
  - Treat every population default as a **prior** and re-anchor it to the runner's
    measured baselines as clean data accrues; require any inferred change to exceed
    the runner's own day-to-day noise before acting [Atkinson 2015]. *Established
    methodologically.*
  - A minority (~8–13%) can show **adverse cardiometabolic** responses to
    appropriate training — monitor per-person and escalate to medical review, never
    "force" a response [Bouchard 2012]. *Pooled-RCT grade.*
- **Hold loosely (unsettled):**
  - "Non-response" as a *fixed trait*: best current evidence says it is largely
    dose/measurement-driven and was abolished in every individual at higher dose,
    but this rests on small, mostly cycling/sedentary cohorts [Montero 2017;
    Pickering 2019]. Probable, not Established — never brand a runner a permanent
    non-responder.
  - **HRV-guided individualization**: plausible and low-risk, but group-mean
    superiority is trivial-to-small and non-significant for VO₂max (SMD +0.13) and
    performance (SMD +0.20) [Manresa-Rocamora 2021]. Use it as opt-in *timing*
    support with modest expectations, not as a proven performance multiplier.
  - Any **individual** trainability estimate or responder classification is
    provisional and inferred only retrospectively — there is no validated genetic
    or blood test to predict it in advance [Pickering 2019; Bonafiglia 2022].

## Coach Directives
- **D1:** Treat every population default (zones, volume rules, paces) as a
  *starting estimate*, and re-anchor it to the runner's own measured baselines as
  soon as ≥2–4 weeks of clean data exist. — confidence: Established
- **D2:** Prescribe intensity from the runner's individual anchors
  (threshold/HRmax/easy-pace), not generic %-of-max bands, once those anchors are
  known. — confidence: Established
- **D3:** Never declare a runner a "non-responder" from a flat trend. First run
  the ladder — dose, measurement noise, recovery/life load, modality fit — in that
  order. — confidence: Probable
- **D4:** When fitness is plateauing and the stimulus is sub-threshold for this
  runner, raise the dose (volume first, then intensity) *progressively* within
  load-ramp limits before concluding anything. — confidence: Probable
- **D5:** Require any inferred change (new baseline, response, regression) to
  exceed the runner's own day-to-day variation before acting; ignore single-
  reading swings. — confidence: Established
- **D6:** State confidence honestly: speak plainly when on population-Established
  science, and explicitly flag when extrapolating to *this* individual ("I'm still
  learning how you respond"). — confidence: Established
- **D7 (opt-in, Stage 2+):** If reliable morning HRV exists, bias key quality
  sessions to days at/above the runner's rolling lnRMSSD baseline and swap in
  easy/recovery when clearly suppressed — framed as timing help with modest
  expected benefit. — confidence: Emerging
- **D8 (safety):** If tracked cardiometabolic markers (e.g. resting BP) worsen
  persistently beyond noise, flag for medical review rather than intensifying
  training. — confidence: Established
- **D9:** Do not promise an outcome magnitude from population averages or any
  "genetic potential"; individual ceilings are unknown in advance and inferred
  only from observed response. — confidence: Established

  the runner's measured baselines. The body's `@daud/core` quantities (`trainabilityEstimate`
  / `individualBaseline` / `hrvBaseline` / `responseTracking`) name the personal-anchor
  computations this policy would drive; several are "not yet computed" and none is a Healthee
  `derived_daily` field today.
- **Where it plugs in:** wherever a threshold is quoted (zones, ramp rates, taper length, HRV
  baseline), this note requires the coach to (a) start from the population default and *say
  so* on cold start, (b) re-anchor to the runner's own baseline once ≥2–4 weeks of clean data
  exist, and (c) require any inferred change to exceed the runner's own day-to-day noise
  before acting. This is the corpus-wide rationale for Healthee's "score vs the person's own
  baseline, not a population norm" honesty stance (see `recovery_readiness`,
  `heart_rate_variability`).
